# Contact point and swing timing

## What this proves

Baseball Savant publishes three per-swing numbers on its Swing Timing and Miss
Distance leaderboard but exposes them only in aggregate: how far along the bat
contact was (x, inches, tied up negative and flail positive), how early or late
the swing was (y, milliseconds, late negative and early positive), and how far
the ball sat off the swing plane (z, inches, over negative and under positive).
This notebook recovers Savant's own definitions empirically from the whiff tails
it does publish per swing, reconstructs all three for every tracked MLB swing in
`savant_data`, and checks the result against Savant's published per-player and
league aggregates on two seasons nothing here was fit on.

Nothing is written to the database. Every cell reads cached files under `data/`.

## Where it stands

Mean absolute error on the pitcher category rates, per player with at least 100
swings. Only 2025 is fit on.

| table | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| pitcher rates | 0.0203 | 0.0205 | 0.0209 |
| pitcher by contact type | 0.0267 | 0.0259 | 0.0283 |
| batter rates | 0.0369 | 0.0359 | 0.0369 |
| batter by contact type | 0.0428 | 0.0412 | 0.0436 |

The holdouts score as well as the season everything was fit on, so nothing is
overfit. The calibration takes its band masses from the 2025 PITCHER split CSV, so
that one table is the fit target and is flagged in the scorecard. The batter
tables improve by the same amount, but they are not fully independent: the two
CSVs describe the same swings, so the LEAGUE totals agree by construction there
too. The per-player error is the out-of-target evidence, and by contact type it
falls from 0.085 to 0.042.

## Axis convention

Savant's, and it is fixed. Plots label the categories in Jeremy's words.

| Axis | Savant field | Units | Sign | Category band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | `delta_batball_tiedup_neg_x2` | inches | tied up negative, flail positive | centered within +/- 4 in |
| y | `delta_batball_late_neg_y2_msec` | ms | late negative, early positive | on time within +/- 7 ms |
| z | `ball_pos_above_plane` | inches | over negative, under positive | lined up within +/- 2 in |

Perfect is contact and centered and on time and lined up. Flawed is a whiff that
is none of the three.

## Caveats

- **The tails are selection-biased.** Savant publishes the ten worst whiffs per
  list per player-season, chosen on the label itself. They identify functional
  form, sign and scale. Nothing that ships is centered or scaled on them, and
  agreement on the tails is a form check, never a generalization check.
- **Contact swings are validated in aggregate only.** Savant publishes no
  per-swing value for a ball in play or a foul, so the only check available for
  them is the rate tables and the histograms.
- **2026 is a partial season.** Its snapshot date is printed in the scorecard
  header and in the notebook's first cell.
- **About 30 percent of swings carry no launch data.** Those contact swings have
  no outcome to invert on an axis that needs one, and are reported as Unknown
  rather than being counted as centered or lined up.

## What the extra signal bought

The whiff models started on 26 geometry features. Four sources were added and
measured one at a time, on the 2024 and 2026 holdouts, as the mean absolute error
of the pitcher and batter category rates:

| feature set | 2024 pitcher | 2026 pitcher | 2024 batter | 2026 batter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| geometry only | 0.0204 | 0.0210 | 0.0373 | 0.0376 |
| plus the re-pull's pitch columns | 0.0203 | 0.0208 | 0.0371 | 0.0373 |
| plus per-batter traits | 0.0204 | 0.0209 | 0.0368 | 0.0367 |
| plus MLB height and weight | 0.0205 | 0.0209 | 0.0372 | 0.0373 |
| plus Savant's swing path | 0.0203 | 0.0209 | 0.0383 | 0.0369 |
| all four | 0.0203 | 0.0209 | 0.0367 | 0.0366 |

Per-batter traits are the largest single source and the only one that clearly
helps the batter view. Biography is worth nothing on its own. The swing path is
mixed alone and pays in company: `avg_batter_x_position` is the ninth strongest
feature on x by gain, and it is the first thing in the model that says where the
batter was standing rather than where the ball was.

**The widened set goes to x only.** Given it, every centered scatter improves:
2024 pitcher slope 0.738 to 0.755 with r 0.795 to 0.799 and error 0.0264 to
0.0262, 2026 pitcher slope 0.715 to 0.738 with r 0.748 to 0.757 and error 0.0300
to 0.0290, and the batter error falls from 0.0504 to 0.0489 and from 0.0504 to
0.0476. Given it, z goes backwards on the batter side on BOTH holdouts: lined-up
r falls 0.745 to 0.708 in 2024 and 0.726 to 0.714 in 2026, with the error rising
in step. So z keeps the twenty-six geometry features and x takes all eighty, and
no holdout scatter is worse than it was. `traits.WIDENED_AXES` is the switch and
carries the numbers.

Three further ideas were built, measured and left off, because each made a holdout
worse:

| idea | what happened |
|---|---|
| z from a plane-height label, ball height at the intercept minus Savant's z | a wash: 2026 pitcher error 0.0209 to 0.0206, 2024 0.0201 to 0.0202 |
| contact-side z from the all-swing model instead of the launch-angle inversion | 2024 batter error 0.0371 to 0.0385, r 0.658 to 0.633 |
| contact-side x sign from spray angle instead of the intercept median | 2024 pitcher r 0.878 to 0.856 |

The code for all three is in the harness, not in the pipeline.

## Where the result falls short

- **Per-player rates are attenuated, and correcting it would make them worse.**
  The scatters have a slope well below 1: a pitcher Savant has near 0.75 lined up
  comes out near 0.68, one near 0.45 comes out near 0.48. That is per-swing error
  doing what per-swing error does. Measured on 2025, with the error that stretching
  each rate about the league mean until the slope is exactly 1 would produce:

  | view | rate | slope | r | mean abs error | error at slope 1 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | pitcher | lined up | 0.724 | 0.824 | 0.0362 | 0.0401 |
  | pitcher | centered | 0.681 | 0.754 | 0.0276 | 0.0354 |
  | pitcher | on time | 0.921 | 0.917 | 0.0197 | 0.0214 |
  | batter | lined up | 0.604 | 0.752 | 0.0438 | 0.0549 |
  | batter | centered | 0.930 | 0.661 | 0.0485 | 0.0519 |
  | batter | on time | 0.776 | 0.708 | 0.0279 | 0.0352 |

  The last column is worse than the one before it on all six. With a correlation of
  0.66 to 0.92 rather than 1, the least-error estimate IS the shrunk one, so a
  slope below 1 is what a noisy estimate should do rather than a fault in it.
  Raising the slope and the accuracy together needs a better per-swing z, not a
  rescale of the rates it produces.

  The per-swing form was built and measured as well. `calibrate.fit_reliability`
  scales every swing about its own player's season mean, with a floor of 50 swings
  below which a player is left alone; Savant's leaderboard has no qualifier to
  borrow, its endpoint is called with min=1 and returns players with one swing. The
  k that puts the pitcher slope at 1 is 0.45 on x, 0.75 on y and 0.90 on z, and it
  is not applied. It moves the level of every player's rate far more than it moves
  the spread between players: pitcher category error goes from 0.020 to 0.079 on
  the 2024 holdout, whiff centered from 0.404 to 0.699 against Savant's 0.402, and
  the pooled x histogram shifts by up to 0.186 in a single bin against the 0.005
  the change was allowed. The function is tested and nothing calls it.
- **x and z are modeled, not recovered.** The discovery ladder cleared its gates
  (r-squared 0.8 and label agreement 0.9) on the timing axis only. See
  `data/reports/discovery_2025.md` for what each axis is and what the published
  fields cannot reach.
- **The contact-type split is closed.** Spec section 6 item 3 names the targets and
  every cell now meets them. 2025 pitcher, league:

  | cell | pooled map | per-type map | Savant |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | in play, lined up | 0.890 | 0.972 | 0.975 |
  | whiff, lined up | 0.259 | 0.196 | 0.194 |
  | in play, centered | 0.750 | 0.763 | 0.763 |
  | foul, centered | 0.503 | 0.726 | 0.726 |
  | whiff, centered | 0.686 | 0.404 | 0.402 |
  | in play, on time | 0.806 | 0.816 | 0.818 |
  | foul, on time | 0.647 | 0.661 | 0.661 |
  | whiff, on time | 0.406 | 0.374 | 0.374 |

  Foul centered was the last one open, and it was arithmetic rather than
  calibration. One foul ball in eight carries no exit velocity, so the x inversion
  had nothing to invert and the swing was reported Unknown rather than centered:
  0.726 times the 0.875 of fouls Statcast measures is the 0.635 that cell used to
  read. Those swings are now scored by `x_full_geometry`, the discovery ladder's
  own winner for x, which is an all-swing formula and needs no exit velocity. z
  cannot follow, because its winner is whiff-only, so those rows stay Unknown on
  that axis and the z cells still carry their known share.
- **The pooled map was worse than no calibration at all on one cell.** Raw, before
  any map, in-play lined up is 0.9753 against Savant's 0.9751. The pooled map moved
  it to 0.838. Fitting three contact types onto one marginal does not just fail to
  help, it destroys agreement the reconstruction already had.
- **Per-player histogram agreement beats the pooled map now.** An earlier version
  of this section reported the opposite, and it was reading Savant's bin labels as
  lower edges when Savant labels a bin by its CENTRE, so every binned comparison
  was half a bin out. Mean per-player Wasserstein-1 over the 2025 pitchers, in axis
  units:

  | axis | raw | pooled map | per-type map |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | x | 3.403 | 0.628 | 0.551 |
  | y | 0.879 | 0.728 | 0.784 |
  | z | 0.442 | 0.208 | 0.229 |

  x improves on the pooled map and y and z give up a little. That is the whole
  price, and it is far smaller than it looked: the figures of 0.933, 1.111 and
  0.478 this file used to carry were the misread grid, not the method.

  The category rates cost nothing either. Correcting the convention was expected to
  cost about 0.001 on them and cost nothing measurable, because that 0.001 was a
  second bug the correction exposed rather than a property of the convention.
  Centring moves the z threshold off a bin edge and into the middle of a bin, and a
  straddling bin carries mass into two bands in a ratio no scaling can change,
  which capped in-play lined up at 0.8979 against the 0.9751 asked for. Cutting the
  target grid at the thresholds removes the cap.
- **The evidence for the centre convention is Savant agreeing with Savant.** Read
  as centres, the pooled 2025 pitcher histogram's three band masses ARE that
  leaderboard's three category rates, to 0.0000 on y, 0.0032 on z and 0.0165 on x,
  the last carrying the known 0.15 percent count gap between that histogram and
  n_swings. Read as lower edges the same three gaps are 0.0317, 0.0864 and 0.0734.
  One offset, three axes, no free parameter.
- **Perfect and flawed are joint categories and a marginal map has no direct hold
  on them.** They land close anyway, which says the three axes are mostly right on
  the same swings rather than right one at a time: in-play perfect 0.634 against
  Savant's 0.629, whiff flawed 0.301 against 0.287. The flawed overshoot is the one
  place the joint rate is visibly off.
- **The x loss curve is extrapolated past its last published point.** The Driveline
  curve stops at a 40 mph exit velocity deficit, which is 14 inches from the sweet
  spot. 5.35 percent of the inversion rows are past it, and clamping stacked 12,221
  of 2025's swings on exactly 14 inches, a spike a monotone map cannot spread. The
  curve now continues at the slope of its final segment. That is not a claim about
  a bat 20 inches off the sweet spot: the quantile map sets the scale afterwards,
  and what the continuation preserves is rank order. The largest group of swings
  now sharing one x value is 6,055 at exactly zero, which is contact at or above
  the exit velocity ceiling. That is a real mode rather than a curve artifact, but
  it is still a clip, the deficit being floored at zero, and a monotone map cannot
  spread it either.
- **Our run value per bin is sharper at zero than Savant's on x and z, and that is
  structural.** On contact swings our x is a function of the exit velocity deficit
  and our z of launch angle minus attack angle, so the best-hit balls land at
  exactly zero by construction and carry their run value with them: the x bin at
  zero averages about +0.08 runs per swing against Savant's +0.02, and z about
  +0.10 against +0.09. Savant's x and z come from bat tracking, independent of the
  outcome, so a centered swing there can still produce a weak result and the curve
  is smoother. The heatmaps inherit this on the contact side; the whiff side does
  not, because whiff x and z come from the fallback model trained on Savant's own
  labels and carry no outcome.
- **The bat's own position at contact is not published.** Statcast gives the
  intercept point's lateral offset and depth but no height, which is the missing
  half of both the x and the z difference.
